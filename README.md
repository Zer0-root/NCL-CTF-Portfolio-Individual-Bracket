# NCL-CTF-Portfolio-Individual-Bracket
This is a repository documenting the author's results from the National Cyber League's Individual CTF bracket. 

I have had a long journey in cybersecurity and initially became interested in the field from observing the effects of malware on computers growing up in the early 2000s. The first computer virus that I witnessed was a remote access trojan attacking a Windows Vista machine at home. I remember the panic that the intrusion caused my family and how strange the idea of computer compromise was. Throughout my educational experience, from grade school to undergraduate college courses, there were very few classes available that I could take on the complexities of cybersecurity. Cybersecurity CTFs have been a guiding light in my self-education and provided direct exposure to extremely technical, industry relevant tasks that were not commonly taught in educational pipelines. 

The National Cyber League's CTF crucible was an interesting journey through various domains of cybersecurity - from web exploitation to forensics - and everything in between. Comparatively to other CTF's I have completed, the primary difference was the strict timing on the window for players to solve challenges and the brutal accuracy rating per question. The gauntlet the NCL provided asked a question that was different than most: "Can you both solve the challenge and solve the challenge correctly on the first attempt?" Participants had to balance the dichotomy of both speed and precision. 

The most memorable aspect of the CTF experience was a multifarious forensic challenge I was assigned during the Team portion where I had to dissect the internals of a Kali Linux virtual machine and identify a backdoor script that could be utilized to achieve a privilege escalation to a user account. The conclusion of the exercise was collecting artifact fragments of banner messages distributed throughout the machine and creating a novel python script to compile and execute the fragments in tandem to produce the flag. The intricacy of the challenge requiring privilege escalation techniques, traversal of multiple directories, and unique python scripting to produce the flag made the experience remarkable.
